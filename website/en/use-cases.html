How Nhill is used day to day

Frequent situations in shops and SMEs, and what Nhill changes. These are examples of how it works, not customer testimonials.

A shop with one register and one salesperson

The situation. The owner is not always in the shop. In the evening, they count the money and compare it with a notebook.

With Nhill. The salesperson rings up sales on Nhill POS with their own account. At closing, they count the drawer; Nhill computes the expected cash and records the variance. The owner reviews the session and the Z report from Nhill Business, even remotely.

What helps. Discounts above the limit and voids need a manager's approval. The activity log keeps every operation.

A hardware store or depot selling at the counter and on invoice

The situation. Individuals pay at the counter; building sites and companies buy on a quote, then an invoice, and pay in instalments, in francs or dollars.

With Nhill. The counter goes through Nhill POS, business customers through quote, invoice, then partial payments in Nhill Business. Both share the same stock. Customer receivables are shown per currency.

What helps. Low-stock alerts before running out; CSV catalogue import to start quickly.

A service provider or sole trader

The situation. Quotes and invoices made in a word processor, payments tracked from memory.

With Nhill. A simple single-user space: quotes, PDF invoices in your name, payments received, expenses with receipts, and the list of what each customer owes you.

An SME with several employees and an accountant

The situation. Several people sell and buy; the accountant receives the documents late.

With Nhill. Each employee has a suitable role (salesperson, manager, accountant reading reports). Reports download as CSV and PDF. The activity log shows who issued, collected or changed what.

VAT-registered business? Your standardised invoices must be issued by an electronic fiscal device recognised by the DGI. Nhill does not replace it: it helps you manage sales, stock and payments, and keeps the reference of each standardised invoice.
